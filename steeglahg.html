Chapter One

Band of the Staglights

SteegLahg of the Staglights came from another realm.

His story is long in the tooth—and a gnarly snaggle of a tooth it is.

SteegLahg had one black tooth tucked in the back of an otherwise handsome, healthy smile. He was born in the Forest of the Staglights. The Staglights were a band of tree-and-bush dwellers from the southern reaches of the realm, known for their cunning tricks and their ability to move with remarkable stealth. These talents made them excellent thieves.

They were also known for crafting fine furniture from the forest's trees, though they seemed to loathe the entire process. The Staglights preferred to use trees themselves as furniture and considered the chairs, tables, and cabinets of the Humanis profoundly silly.

Silly, however, was profitable.

Furniture provided the band with a living they could not otherwise attain—at least not legally.

The Corporation

Steegs had become skilled with his Leatherman and belt tools. His apprenticeship had blossomed into full-time work.

SteegLahg was gathering unique pieces of wood for Wilson’s new daughter’s room. Wilson was one of the few Humanis Steegs tolerated contact with. His property sat well south of the Staglights quadrant, though not south enough for most of the band.

Along the property line, just east of Staglight land, SteegLahg noticed etchings in the trunks of the pines. A seagull. The initials HT.

This land had been up for auction many years ago and purchased by an unknown. The Staglights had been in no place to offer any price during the lean, dry years.

Then there it was: straight as an arrow, not a knot in sight. A beauty, for sure. Oak was wonderful for furniture. Its strength, durability, and striking grain patterns were what Steegs called baklahzee.

The shit.

Steegs whipped out his handmade axe and quickly struck the bottom of the tree, marking an S-shaped pattern. He stepped back.

Then he noticed it.

“Fuck me,” Steegs said aloud.

He was standing at the very edge of Staglight land. On the far side of his S marking, at the base of the tree, was a seagull.

HT.

Whatever HT was, it had marked the tree before Steegs ever put his own S into the bark.

He processed the oak in silence and stacked the wood beside the stump. It was still good wood. Beautiful wood. But it no longer felt like his wood.

By the time he returned home, Steegs had a game plan. He would find out who HT was. He would ask around Helthesbroad. The tavern regulars, the trade-store people, Winston Gallroy—somebody would know who had been buying timber near Staglight land.

But first, he would speak to Folstein.

Chapter Thirteen

Folstein

Staglights do not give gifts after childhood.

They grant favors.

And favors are taken seriously.

To refuse a reasonable favor is to declare yourself an outright enemy. To fail to pay back a favor within a reasonable time lowers your status among the Staglights. One who repeatedly fails to repay—or re-favor—another eventually earns the name stoober.

A stoober lives a hard life in the Staglight community.

One must note: it works both ways. And the word reasonable is often where the problems begin.

A Staglight who repays debts well, and chooses how to repay them wisely, can gain great status over the years. Wealth, respect, good trees—all of it can follow.

Beware befriending a stight person if you are not one hundred percent certain of their intentions. A stight person can favor you with something you could never repay, and then you lose status trying.

Many Staglights have gone treeless because of such practices. A wealthy stight may offer a piece of farm equipment, for instance—something a poorer Staglight could never hope to return in kind. The favor is accepted. The debt remains. The trees eventually change hands.

Now, there are rules about who may favor whom. You must be seen together as acquaintances more than once. Invitations must be accepted. Birthdays attended. The Staglight community is very astute about such things, however, and generally keeps minor skirmishes between the involved parties.

The next morning, Steegs headed north-northwest, down through the gully off his property, toward his neighbor Folstein.

Folstein was older than most Staglights. Much older.

He had been around for many, many years.

And he was still an excellent axe man indeed.

Steegs could hear him before he saw him.

Stoig!

The axe struck oak.

Stoig!

Folstein lived in a cove below the hill, near an old tree house and a small shed where he kept his wood, tools, and things that did not belong in a tree. His hair was reddish-gray and hung down his back. He looked thin from a distance—almost frail to a Humanis eye—but Folstein could split a log with one swing that would take three ordinary Staglights to move.

Folstein stopped chopping as Steegs came down the hill. He did not turn around.

“Your boots say staflaross,” Folstein said.

Steegs looked at the axe in Folstein’s hand, then at the woodpile.

“I found a mark.”

Folstein became still.

“A seagull,” Steegs said. “And HT.”

For a moment, nothing moved except the trees.

Then Folstein set down his axe.

“SteegLahg,” he said quietly, “the logs whisper to me when they are about to crack. Do not hold back what you saw. What you do next may be what holds the key.”

Staglight Traits

Certain ways of the Staglights do not translate neatly to Humanis custom.

Tickle

For Staglights, tickling is not merely a children’s game or a Humanis joke. It is a familiar kind of relief: part massage, part stretch, and part release. A good tickle can settle the body like a satisfying bowel movement or a well-timed bit of flatulence. Staglights tickle one another often—to reconnect, to calm a friend, and sometimes because a stressful moment has made them nervous and they do not know what else to do.

Pinky Strength

Staglights possess remarkable strength in their pinky fingers and pinky toes. The smallest digits are not an afterthought in their world; they grip, brace, pull, and hold with a power that surprises nearly every Humanis who witnesses it.

Staglight Details

Small facts worth keeping in the record.

Furniture

Staglights prefer trees as furniture, but learned that Humanis furniture can keep soup in the pot.

Silver, Smoke & Port

SteegLahg has a penchant for silver. He is only seen with a long pipe or port wine—or so the record says.

Favors

After childhood, Staglights grant favors rather than gifts. A favor must be repaid reasonably, or status can be lost.

The Log Rule

Good listeners know wood speaks before it cracks. Folstein considers this practical attention, not magic.

Places

The places that have already begun to shape the story.

The Rhelm

A giant supercontinent joining almost all of Earth’s land. It formed about 335 million years ago and began breaking apart roughly 175 million years ago, creating the modern continents.

The southern forest realm where Steegs was born: tree houses, bush dwellers, furniture work, and old boundaries.

The Gully

The north-northwest descent from Steegs’ property to Folstein’s cove, tree house, shed, and woodpile.

Helthesbroad

A Humanis settlement for tobacco, salt, iron nails, trade, tavern talk, and bad opinions.

Sublevel B

Not seen, but invoked in literature and during court proceedings. Owner of the centuries-old corporation currently purchasing timber in Staglight areas.

Riley

A Humanis who is friendly to Staglights as long as he is drunk and has some edge or upper hand. Trusted by neither Staglights nor Humanis.

Winston Gallroy

A fat, well-dressed Humanis who owns furniture galleries in three neighboring settlements. He likes Staglight work because customers like to say their tables were made by forest people. He pays for skill, but not always fairly.

Honest Tom’s Corporation

A seagull-marked corporation whose timber, contracts, and Maintenance Department have reached the forest. During Pangea, its kin were in the subtropical northern hemisphere—roughly today’s Mediterranean or North African latitude, in the land now called Finland—before migrating to the current-day Minnesota and South Dakota area.
