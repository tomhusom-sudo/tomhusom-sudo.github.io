SteegLahg of the Staglights

Working Manuscript — Book One: The Seagull Mark

This is the working manuscript: a living draft of the story. Chapters One, Twelve, and Thirteen sit closest to the current story page; the paths between them are still being explored.

CHAPTER One

Band of the Staglights

SteegLahg of the Staglights came from another realm.

His story is long in the tooth-and a gnarly snaggle of a tooth it is.

SteegLahg had one black tooth tucked in the back of an otherwise handsome, healthy smile. He was born in the Forest of the Staglights. The Staglights were a band of tree-and-bush dwellers from the southern reaches of the realm, known for their cunning tricks and their ability to move with remarkable stealth. These talents made them excellent thieves.

They were also known for crafting fine furniture from the forest's trees, though they seemed to loathe the entire process. The Staglights preferred to use trees themselves as furniture and considered the chairs, tables, and cabinets of the Humanis profoundly silly.

Silly, however, was profitable.

Furniture provided the band with a living they could not otherwise attain-at least not legally.

CHAPTER Two

Bosoko

Bosoko lived in a bush house beneath the tree house where Katah kept Steegs, Tah, and Tel from falling out of the world. He was single, nervous, and made flutes for Staglights and Humanis alike. He also tended Katah's garden, repaired her roof, and brought home odd goods from the forest people whenever he had been kind enough to earn them.

Bosoko and Katah had developed a relationship they hid badly. Everyone knew. Steegs knew. Tah knew. Tel especially knew, and Tel did not care for Bosoko one bit. Still, most nights Bosoko ate dinner with them, and no one said much about it because the soup was better when he had visited town.

Bosoko did not talk like a hero. He talked like a person who had spent his life keeping little things from breaking. Steegs watched him repair a cracked chair one evening and wondered why a Staglight would go to such trouble for a Humanis idea. Bosoko only shrugged. 'Silly is profitable,' he said. 'And profitable keeps soup in the pot.'

After meals, Bosoko sometimes sat outside the bush house with a long pipe and a glass of port. Steegs loved the aroma, ember, smoke rings, and the whole thing. Bosoko’s Humanis tobacco was finer and more pungent than ordinary Staglight tobacco—frowned upon in the forest, but traded like a scarce commodity. Steegs began taking tiny pinches for the pipe he had not carved yet.

CHAPTER Three

Citra's Tree

There was one tree nobody sat beneath anymore. It belonged to Citra, Steegs' sister, though Citra had been gone long enough that the bark had grown over the first marks of her name.

Katah kept the little clearing tidy. Tel pretended he did not notice. Tah noticed everything and said almost nothing. Steegs had learned that grief in a Staglight family was not a thing you threw away. It was a piece of furniture you kept moving around because nobody could decide where it belonged.

That morning, Steegs left a small flute shaving at the base of Citra's tree. Bosoko had made it. He told nobody why.

CHAPTER Four

The First Favor

The first favor Steegs accepted was a bad one, though it did not look bad at first. A Staglight named Brim offered him a silver compass found in a Humanis trader's cart. Steegs loved a good compass. He loved the little needle, the clean circle, and the way it acted as though the whole world could be understood if you only held it flat enough.

'What do you want for it?' Steegs asked.

Brim smiled. 'Nothing today.'

Folstein heard about it before Steegs could hide the compass. He made Steegs return it the next morning. 'Nothing today,' Folstein said, 'is the most expensive kind of nothing.'

CHAPTER Five

Helthesbroad

Helthesbroad was a Humanis settlement where tobacco, salt, iron nails, and bad opinions could all be found in abundance. Steegs disliked the pavement most of all. It was flat, dead, and had no proper place for moss.

Folstein ran into town for the families in the gully. He knew which shopkeepers would trade fairly and which ones would smile while making you poorer. Steegs came along because he wanted tobacco and because a young Staglight who stayed home too long became a nuisance to everyone nearby.

At the tavern they met Riley, a Humanis who was friendly as long as he was drunk and had some small edge over the person he was speaking to. Riley knew something about timber buyers east of the forest, but he would not yet say what it was.

CHAPTER Six

Otis the BarkMole

Otis found Steegs before Steegs found Otis. She came from under a hollow furniture log with bark-dark fur, twiggy whiskers, and a small permanent sneer that made her look meaner than she was.

She was a female barkmole, stout as a small dog and twice as determined. She stole one of Steegs' gloves, then followed him home, then refused to leave. Katah called her a nuisance. Tel called her a rat. Bosoko quietly made her a little sleeping box with a hole cut into the side.

Otis slept inside Steegs' tool belt the first night. By morning, she had chewed through the strap that had been rubbing his hip for months. After that, he considered her hired.

— ✦ —

CHAPTER Seven

Winston Gallroy

Winston Gallroy was fat, well dressed, and owned furniture galleries in three neighboring Humanis settlements. Folstein called him a business contact. Steegs called him greedy before he had even met him.

Gallroy liked Staglight work because Humanis customers liked to say their tables were made by forest people. He paid for skill, but not always fairly. Folstein knew every trick in Gallroy's book and still traded with him, which confused Steegs.

'You do not have to trust a man to know his price,' Folstein said. 'You only have to know when he is trying to make you forget yours.'

— ✦ —

CHAPTER Eight

The Tools of a Staglight

Steegs became skilled with his handmade axe, his belt tools, and the Leatherman he had acquired under circumstances he did not explain. His apprenticeship blossomed into full-time work.

He loved a good smoke, a good glass of tree port, a nice compass, and a job that ended with something useful where there had only been a tree. He hated controlled organizations, large companies, large Humanis machines, concrete, pavement, and being told to hurry by anyone who had never cut a board straight in their life.

Folstein taught him that the best wood was not always the biggest tree. Bosoko taught him that the best repair was the one nobody noticed. Between them, Steegs learned to make furniture that even a Staglight could admit was not entirely silly.

— ✦ —

CHAPTER Nine

Treeless

A poorer Staglight named Noll accepted a favor from a wealthy stight: a farm machine too valuable to refuse and too costly to repay. For a while Noll looked lucky. Then the visits began. Then the requests. Then the trees changed hands.

Steegs watched Noll become a stoober in slow motion. Nobody called him one at first. The Staglight community was too polite for that. They simply stopped inviting him to certain meals. They stopped asking what he thought. They looked at his empty stretch of forest and changed the subject.

Folstein told Steegs that a favor could save a person or ruin one. The difference was not generosity. The difference was whether the person offering it wanted you standing afterward.

— ✦ —

CHAPTER Ten

The Gully

Folstein's cove sat below Steegs' property, where the trees leaned toward a little house built in branches and a shed full of tools. Folstein was more than two hundred years old, thin as a root, reddish-gray haired, and sharper with an axe than any Staglight Steegs had seen.

Sometimes Folstein spoke as though the logs had told him things. Steegs thought this was old-man nonsense until Folstein named a storm before it came, found a cracked trunk before it fell, and warned Steegs away from a trail that later filled with Humanis surveyors.

'The logs whisper to me when they are about to crack,' Folstein said. 'That is not magic. That is listening.'

— ✦ —

CHAPTER Eleven

The Oak

The oak stood just east of Staglight land, straight as an arrow and not a knot in sight. It was a beauty. Oak was wonderful for furniture. Its strength, durability, and striking grain patterns were what Steegs called baklahzee.

The shit.

He knew the boundary was close. He knew the land had been sold long ago, during the lean dry years, to an unknown buyer. Still, the tree looked as though it had been waiting for him. Steegs marked the base with the S-shaped sign of his work and brought up his axe.

— ✦ —

CHAPTER Twelve

The Corporation

SteegLahg was gathering unique pieces of wood for Wilson's new daughter's room. Wilson was one of the few Humanis Steegs tolerated contact with. His property sat well south of the Staglights quadrant, though not south enough for most of the band.

Along the property line, just east of Staglight land, SteegLahg noticed etchings in the trunks of the pines. A seagull. The initials HT.

This land had been up for auction many years ago and purchased by an unknown. The Staglights had been in no place to offer any price during the lean, dry years.

Steegs wondered who HT was-and what they were doing with the trees.

He whipped out his handmade axe and quickly struck the bottom of the oak, marking an S-shaped pattern. He stepped back.

Then he noticed it.

'Fuck me,' Steegs said aloud.

On the far side of his S marking, at the base of the tree, was a seagull. HT.

Whatever HT was, it had marked the tree before Steegs ever put his own S into the bark. He processed the oak in silence and stacked the wood beside the stump. It was still good wood. Beautiful wood. But it no longer felt like his wood.

By the time he returned home, Steegs had a game plan. He would ask around Helthesbroad. The tavern regulars, the trade-store people, Winston Gallroy-somebody would know who had been buying timber near Staglight land.

But first, he would speak to Folstein.

CHAPTER Thirteen

Folstein

Staglights do not give gifts after childhood. They grant favors. And favors are taken seriously.

To refuse a reasonable favor is to declare yourself an outright enemy. To fail to pay back a favor within a reasonable time lowers your status among the Staglights. One who repeatedly fails to repay-or re-favor-another eventually earns the name stoober.

The next morning, Steegs headed north-northwest, down through the gully off his property, toward his neighbor Folstein.

Steegs could hear him before he saw him.

Stoig!

The axe struck oak.

Stoig!

Folstein lived in a cove below the hill, near an old tree house and a small shed where he kept his wood, tools, and things that did not belong in a tree. His hair was reddish-gray and hung down his back. He looked thin from a distance-almost frail to a Humanis eye-but Folstein could split a log with one swing that would take three ordinary Staglights to move.

Folstein stopped chopping as Steegs came down the hill. He did not turn around.

'Your boots say staflaross,' Folstein said.

'I found a mark,' Steegs said.

Folstein became still.

'A seagull. And HT.'

For a moment, nothing moved except the trees.

Then Folstein set down his axe.

'SteegLahg,' he said quietly, 'the logs whisper to me when they are about to crack. Do not hold back what you saw. What you do next may be what holds the key.'
